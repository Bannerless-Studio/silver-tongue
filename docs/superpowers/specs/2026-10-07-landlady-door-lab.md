# Landlady door lab experiment

The tutorial’s job is to make “the world opens through something I did” happen within about three minutes for a beginner who wants to learn but struggles to focus. These are pacing targets, not measured results.

Keep the unfamiliar room, desk, Book, and the rule that comprehension reveals the story. Drop three mandatory papers, compulsory translation of both sides of every exchange, treating one sighting as readiness to recall, and choices that silently do nothing.

## Opening and new words

1. Wake in the unfamiliar room. Enter can finish the crawl.
2. Sound out only the ID name, 김민준: gim, min, jun. The title, birth number, newspaper and bill remain in the Book for later reading. Hearing the name at the door proves the connection.
3. `room-wake/call`: 민준 씨? → 아니요. New: 씨, 아니요 (two items). Asking the name back and silence are also meaningful, advancing choices. The decoded name is highlighted.
4. `room-wake/friend`: 민준 씨 친구예요? → 네. / 아니요. New: 친구, 네, 예요 (two words and one form). Claim friendship and receive a small stall card headed 지우네. Tell the truth and hear 지우 씨, a name plus the already met honorific. Asking about Min-jun again or staying quiet yields a guided ID route. Each choice gets its own reaction and remembered callback.
5. `room-wake/missing`: 민준 씨 어디예요? → 몰라요. New: 어디, 몰라요 (two items). Every alternative and silence also moves on. She points down the alley. Total door budget: six lexical items/chunks plus one form; seven token IDs besides proper names. All words remain glossed. The door says “What does she mean?”; the desk says “Sound it out”.
6. `street-hello/hello`: 안녕하세요 → 안녕하세요. New: 안녕하세요 (one chunk). `idcard`: 민준 씨? → 네. New: none. Every answer, alternative, and silence receives a response and changes Park’s parting gesture.
7. `stall-lead/recognition`: 네 → 네. New: none. The card route matches 지우네 above the counter; decoding it in the Book reveals the stall name and makes recognition visible. The heard-name route says 지우 씨? and receives a warm smile and an invitation beside the counter. Both provide a place to talk while she serves, without deciding her relationship to Min-jun. That reveal remains in stall-family.
8. Return to Park for street-introductions. Include this visit in the action invariant because it can fall within five minutes. New items per exchange: `park` — 박 (name), 할아버지; `who` — 누구세요, 입니다; `ask` — 이름, 뭐, 저, 는; `sit` — 앉으세요, 감사합니다; `understand` — 한국어, 알아요; `bye` — 안녕히, 계세요, 가세요 (form of 가요). All replies, alternatives, and silence have consequences at the visit’s end. Later word chains retain their original order.

## Reward loop

Use a stall card, not a photograph. Its heading 지우네 means Ji-woo’s place: a natural proper-name sign consistent with Ji-woo running the existing stall. It avoids introducing 김밥 before its food lesson. The sign’s ending is decoded as writing, not tested as a new conversational word.

Claiming friendship gives something to decode. The Book’s Papers tab uses the existing desk-style syllable reader; it does not print the unread card’s romanization beside the entry. Finishing the heading persists recognition, reveals the place name, and changes the stall’s arrival text. Finishing it at the stall pays off immediately too. Honesty gives something to hear: 지우 씨, played with the existing Ji-woo and honorific word clips. Saying it at the stall earns personal recognition and a warm invitation. Neither reward interrupts her work or buys privileged attention over the other.

All other options still receive a distinct response and a later visible change. They are not retry loops. The complete map below is the authored contract: every reaction appears immediately, every consequence at that scene’s end. Choices at the friend exchange also determine the stall welcome. No action is graded through its narration.

Acceptance belongs to scene and line IDs, with visible-word fragments and essential distinctions. Pronouns are not globally removed. Negation cannot be added or dropped. Equally matching reply slips ask for an explicit choice.

## Experiment boundary and saved progress

Runtime reactions, accepted alternatives, silence completion, earned papers, decoded-card recognition, heard-name handling and stall branching all use the same `doorExperimentOn` / `labMode()` boundary. The main page follows its fixed ID/name route and never reads, clears or writes opening choices or grants the card.

This experiment alone uses the single `DOOR_LAB_PREFIX` constant in `packages/quiet-web/src/storage.ts`: `silver-tongue-lab-door:`. Every session, setting, desk position, paper record, hint, counter and choice record passes through it. The older `silver-tongue-lab:` namespace and main-site storage are neither loaded nor changed. This is experiment isolation, not a positional-save migration.

The versioned per-game opening record stores `options` by `scene:exchange` and `cardRead` / per-game `cardAt`. It replaces the old Boolean friend record; legacy booleans are ignored. Reload retains every selected option, the earned card, decoded recognition and callbacks for an unfinished scene. Core’s current run and five recent talks cannot serve as a durable choice record.

Core 0.19’s accepted `AltOutcome` advances an alternate reply. The lab course configures all opening alts with `accept: true`; the lab supplies English reactions and callbacks. Core’s `confused` input only repeats the line, and tiles cannot express an alternate. For silence and those tile cases, the lab sends a valid completion input, keeps the actual chosen option in its record, and echoes the actual slip or “…”. Core’s accepted action is never presented as the player’s choice. Shared content and main-page core rules are unchanged.

Before a real merge, saved dialogue positions must map by scene and exchange ID, handling removed exchanges explicitly. Partial desk progress must map by paper line and character, rather than an old numeric syllable position. That migration is deferred and is not implemented here.

## Content boundary

`content/settings/seoul-city/opening.json` owns the scene styles, choice map above, reaction/consequence message IDs, reward paper and place, directions, and arrival branches. The builder validates those references. Heard-key `audioWords` refer to existing content words; the builder resolves their clips in declared order and removes the word references from the compiled opening profile. Runtime plays the profile’s clips without Korean text, word IDs or setting-specific IDs in frontend source. No new audio is required by this refactor.

Line-keyed meaning rules live in `content/learner/en/scribe-ko.json`. The builder checks current lines and exact meanings for drift; shared view code reads the compiled rules. Door glossing, slip selection and the meaning prompt are also declared in content, rather than selected by a scene-name comparison in UI code.

## Success test

Watch five easily distracted beginners, individually, without coaching, with permission to stop. At the door’s end ask what they discovered and where they want to go next. At the stall ask what helped them connect it to the door: the decoded card/sign or the heard name. Stop and revise if three of five cannot name the stall as where to go next. Build checks and exhaustive paths do not replace this playtest.

Automated checks enumerate every option in all four scenes, assert a reaction, advancement, durable choice and visible callback, then walk all 3,072 reply/silence paths through room-wake → street-hello → stall-lead, plus 768 decoded-card variants (3,840 total). Every path must finish; different selections must yield different actual downstream text. The checker sweep retains exact meanings, visible fragments, strict negation and current acceptance keys. See [the audit and audio handoff](2026-10-07-landlady-door-audit.md) for per-site results and verification.
