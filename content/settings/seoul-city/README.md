# Seoul City setting

Year: 2000, Seoul, three years after the IMF crisis. Jobs are scarce, rooms are small, and everyone counts their change.
Tech that exists: landlines, phone booths, pagers, PC bangs, TV.
Tech that doesn't: smartphones, anything that translates. Nobody can look up a word for the player, and nobody translates for them.

## Premise

You fell asleep over a Korean textbook and woke in a stranger's rented room in Seoul, with his ID card on the desk.
He is Kim Min-jun (민준), a physics student; nobody knows where he went. Later stages reveal he swapped places with you.
His older sister Ji-woo (지우) runs a snack stall and feeds you; the landlady wants the rent; Grandpa Park on the bench teaches you.

## Rule for clues

Clues are spoken in the stage's own words, never withheld by plot, only by comprehension. A buried clue is an exchange marked
`"pin": true` (with `"slots": {}`): the player hears it on day one and understands it once they know the words.

Stage 1 clues: the landlady asks 민준 씨 친구예요? (room-wake; saying 네 is a lie that works), says 민준 씨는 삼월까지 냈어요 (room-rent), and Ji-woo,
showing a photo, says 동생이에요. 지금 없어요 (stall-family).

Stage 2 clues (act 2, the accidental investigation): a letter for Min-jun from his university (room-letter); his lab-mate Su-jin says
민준 씨는 삼월부터 안 왔어요 and 민준 씨가 책을 가져갔어요 (campus-labmate); a man he borrowed 십만 원 from says 다음 주에 또 올게요
(room-creditor). The copy shop by the gate (copy-shift) is stage 2's paid work; its small talk brings back the photo and rent words.
